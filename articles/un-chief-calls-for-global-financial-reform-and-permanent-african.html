United Nations (UN) Secretary-General Antonio Guterres yesterday called for sweeping reforms to global governance structures, urging permanent African representation on the UN Security Council and an overhaul of multilateral financial institutions.

"We need a reformed multilateral system that is more representative, more effective and more legitimate," Guterres said during remarks at the event. "This includes permanent African representation on the Security Council and reform of the Bretton Woods institutions."

The Secretary-General’s remarks come at a time when debates over the composition and function of the UN Security Council have intensified. Guterres called for changes to both the Security Council’s membership and the global financial architecture, arguing that institutions established after World War II no longer serve the needs of the 21st century.

"The Bretton Woods institutions need an overhaul," Guterres stated. "We need a new global financial architecture that works for all countries, not just a few."

The call for permanent African representation on the Security Council addresses a long-standing demand by African nations and regional bodies. The continent of 1.4 billion people currently has no permanent seat on the 15-member Security Council, which includes five permanent members — the United States, Russia, China, France, and the United Kingdom — each holding veto power.

Guterres emphasized that reform is not merely about adding seats but about transforming the decision-making process to better reflect global demographics and economic realities. The Secretary-General’s remarks were delivered as part of broader efforts to build consensus among UN member states on governance changes.

The proposal for permanent Security Council seats for Africa would require amendment to the UN Charter, a process that involves negotiation among current permanent members and two-thirds approval of the General Assembly. Several current permanent members have expressed openness to reform in recent years, though details remain under discussion.

Guterres also linked the Security Council reform to broader changes in global financial institutions, arguing that economic governance structures similarly require democratization. The Secretary-General’s comments underscore the interconnected nature of political and financial reform efforts within the United Nations system.

The speech marks the latest development in ongoing discussions about UN reform that have persisted for decades. While there is broad agreement that the current structure requires changes, specifics regarding the number of new seats, eligibility criteria, and implementation timelines remain subjects of active negotiation among member states.
