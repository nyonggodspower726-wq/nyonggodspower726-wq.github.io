A growing number of aggrieved All Progressives Congress members and former party leaders have begun aligning with the Rainbow Coalition led by the Minister of the Federal Capital Territory, Nyesom Wike, in a development that is sharpening internal tensions within Nigeria's ruling party. The realignment underscores widening fractures in the APC ahead of upcoming political contests.

The Rainbow Coalition, positioned as a platform for discontented politicians, has gained traction among APC chieftains who have expressed dissatisfaction with the party's direction. Sources close to the development say the realignment reflects broader dissatisfaction within the ruling party, though the exact motivations for individual defections vary.

Wike, a former governor of Rivers State, has been vocal in his criticism of the Tinubu administration since taking up his current role. His emergence as a rallying point for disaffected APC members adds a new dimension to the party's internal dynamics, particularly as the administration grapples with economic and security challenges. The coalition's growth signals that the APC's cohesion cannot be taken for granted, especially as figures who once occupied prominent party positions now look elsewhere for political relevance.

The trend has been accompanied by public statements from some of the defecting leaders, who have cited a need for alternative political space as their reason for crossing over. While some have joined the coalition voluntarily, others are reported to have been courted by Wike's camp in what observers describe as a strategic effort to expand the coalition's influence. The movement has not been without controversy, with some APC chieftains dismissing the realignments as politically motivated rather than ideologically driven.

Political analysts tracking the development say the defections, while significant, do not yet threaten the APC's hold on power at the federal level. However, the ripple effects are being felt in several states where the party's structure has been tested by the exit of key figures. The opposition Peoples Democratic Party, for its part, has maintained a cautious distance, noting that it welcomes defectors but insisting on proper vetting processes.
