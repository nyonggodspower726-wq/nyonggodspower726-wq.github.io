LAGOS — LG Electronics has introduced a new lineup of Smart TVs tailored for the Nigerian market, combining advanced entertainment features with built-in privacy controls and transparency tools that give users greater visibility into how their data is used.

The announcement, made this week, positions LG as a direct responder to the dual demands of Nigerian consumers: seamless access to streaming content and growing concerns about digital privacy. The new models integrate voice command systems that clearly indicate when audio is being recorded, on-screen dashboards that display real-time data usage, and regional content recommendations powered by artificial intelligence.

"Nigerian consumers are embracing smart home technology at an unprecedented pace," said a company spokesperson in a statement. "Our new TV lineup puts users in control — not just of what they watch, but of how their personal information is handled. That balance between smarter entertainment and enhanced privacy is what sets these models apart."

The television models feature LG's latest AI Picture and AI Sound technologies, which automatically optimize viewing and audio quality based on content type and room environment. A new voice recognition system includes a visible indicator on screen whenever the microphone is active, addressing common concerns about always-listening devices.

Perhaps most significantly for the Nigerian market, the TVs include a localized content discovery engine that surfaces Nigerian films, music and Nollywood productions alongside international streaming options. The system learns viewing preferences over time but gives users the ability to review, delete or opt out of data collection at any point through an on-screen privacy menu.

"Transparency is built into the experience," said Toyosi Falade, a tech analyst based in Lagos. "Users can see exactly what data is being collected, why it is being collected, and have immediate control to stop it. That level of user empowerment is rare in the current smart TV landscape."

The launch also coincides with increased regulatory scrutiny of data practices by technology companies operating in Africa. While LG has not announced specific partnerships with Nigerian regulatory bodies, the company said the new TVs comply with international data protection standards and are designed with features that align with emerging continental guidelines on digital privacy.

Industry observers note that the Nigerian smart TV market has grown significantly over the past three years, driven by falling hardware costs, improved internet infrastructure and the rise of local streaming platforms. LG's new models enter a competitive space that includes brands such as Samsung, Hisense and TCL, all of which are introducing similar privacy-focused features.

The company said pricing and availability details will be revealed in the coming weeks, with the first units expected to reach major retail outlets in Lagos, Abuja and Port Harcourt by the end of the quarter.
