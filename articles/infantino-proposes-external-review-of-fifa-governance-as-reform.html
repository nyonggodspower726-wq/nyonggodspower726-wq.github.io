Gianni Infantino has proposed an external review of how world football's governing body operates, and says he is open to reform. In a letter sent to members of the Fifa Council and all of its national associations, Infantino said the proposal aims to strengthen transparency and address growing criticism of the organisation's independence.

The Fifa president's missive marks a significant development in the ongoing debate about the governance of world football's most powerful body. Infantino, who has led Fifa since 2016, acknowledged that the organisation faces scrutiny on multiple fronts, including questions about its decision-making processes, financial transparency, and democratic legitimacy.

"FIFA's democratic processes, independence and sporting authority were never for sale," Infantino wrote in the letter, which was cited by Goal.com. The statement comes amid repeated calls from various stakeholders for greater oversight and structural changes within the organisation.

The proposal for external review represents a notable shift in tone from Infantino, who has previously resisted calls for independent oversight. By opening the door to external examination, the Fifa president appears to be responding to mounting pressure from member associations, football pundits, and reform advocates who have long argued that the body needs structural changes to maintain credibility.

Infantino's letter to the Fifa Council and national associations signals his willingness to engage with reform demands. The proposal comes at a time when Fifa is already under examination for various aspects of its operations, including hosting rights allocations, financial distribution, and the conduct of its leadership.

The Fifa chief's move to invite external scrutiny could potentially reshape the organisation's internal dynamics. Reform advocates have long argued that independent oversight is essential for maintaining public trust in the sport's governing body, particularly given past controversies that have surrounded Fifa's leadership and decision-making.

As the football world digests this development, the proposal sets the stage for what could be a pivotal moment in Fifa's ongoing efforts to address governance concerns and respond to calls for greater accountability.
