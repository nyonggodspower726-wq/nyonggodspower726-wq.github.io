The African Democratic Congress has warned that rising incidents of political violence, attacks on opposition members and alleged voter intimidation could jeopardise the credibility of the 2027 general elections. The party's National Publicity Secretary issued the warning, stating that the trend, if unchecked, risks undermining the integrity of the electoral process.

The ADC said it had documented multiple cases of opposition members being attacked ahead of the 2027 general elections. The party said the incidents ranged from physical assaults to intimidation tactics deployed at campaign rallies and in constituencies across the country. The ADC warned that without urgent intervention, the credibility of the elections could be called into question both domestically and by international observers.

The development comes at a time when political tension is rising ahead of the 2027 general elections. The opposition party said it was particularly concerned about the safety of its candidates and supporters, noting that the attacks appeared to be coordinated in some instances. The ADC has called on the Independent National Electoral Commission and security agencies to investigate the incidents and ensure that the electoral environment is conducive for free and fair voting.

Reacting to the warning, political analysts said the credibility of the 2027 elections was already a subject of public debate. The ADC's warning adds another layer of concern to an already volatile political landscape. The party's call for investigation was seen as a necessary step toward addressing the security challenges that could affect voter turnout and participation.

The ADC also said it would continue to monitor the situation and would not hesitate to approach relevant authorities if the trend persisted. The party urged well-meaning Nigerians and civil society organisations to speak out against the attacks and to support efforts at ensuring a peaceful transition of power in 2027.

As of the time of the statement, no official response had been received from the Independent National Electoral Commission or the police command on the specific allegations. The ADC said it would provide further updates as the situation develops.
