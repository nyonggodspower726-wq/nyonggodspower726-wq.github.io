The latest Lassa Fever Situation Report, released by the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention, places the case fatality rate at 24 percent — the highest recorded in the current outbreak cycle. The figure represents a notable increase from previous reporting periods and signals a deepening public health crisis across the country.

According to the report, the 1,074 confirmed cases this year have been spread across 24 states. The 258 deaths recorded represent a case fatality ratio that health officials say warrants urgent attention. The report did not specify which states accounted for the majority of fatalities, but the geographic spread underscores the nationwide reach of the current outbreak.

Lassa fever, an acute viral hemorrhagic illness transmitted to humans via contact with food or household items contaminated by rodent urine or feces, continues to pose a significant threat in West Africa. Nigeria typically sees seasonal peaks, but the current numbers have pushed the outbreak into a concerning trajectory for the year. Health authorities have maintained that the disease is preventable through improved sanitation, safe food storage, and community awareness, though the rising fatality rate suggests challenges in containment and treatment access.

The situation report covers epidemiological week 35, which spans the period from August 25 to 31, 2026. During that window, 18 new confirmed cases were identified. The cumulative total of 1,074 cases across the year indicates that the outbreak has already surpassed many previous annual tallies, with several months remaining in 2026.

Public health experts have noted that the case fatality rate for Lassa fever typically ranges between 1 and 15 percent among hospitalized patients, though the overall rate can be higher depending on the timing of treatment, patient age, and access to care. The 24 percent figure reported by the NCDC suggests that a significant proportion of cases are presenting for medical attention at later stages, or that treatment gaps are contributing to poorer outcomes.

State governments and health agencies have been urged to intensify surveillance, improve rapid response mechanisms, and ensure that diagnostic facilities and treatment centers are adequately stocked with supportive care supplies. The NCDC has also emphasized the importance of community-level prevention measures, including proper food storage to deter rodent infestation and early reporting of suspected cases.

As the year progresses, the trajectory of the outbreak will depend on the effectiveness of response efforts, the availability of medical resources, and the degree to which preventive messaging reaches at-risk populations. The current data suggests that without intensified intervention, the death toll could continue to rise before the outbreak is brought under control.
