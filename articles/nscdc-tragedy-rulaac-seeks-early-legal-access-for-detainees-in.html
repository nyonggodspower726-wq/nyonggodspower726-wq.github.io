The deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners while in the custody of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps in Niger State have triggered the inauguration of a 10-member investigative panel and renewed demands for early legal access to detainees across Nigerian security agencies, rights groups said Tuesday.

The panel was inaugurated on Tuesday by the Niger State government to investigate the circumstances surrounding the miners' deaths, according to The Nation Newspaper. The panel comprises 10 members tasked with determining the conditions that led to the fatalities, which have raised fresh questions about the safety of people held by Nigerian security agencies.

The Rights Lawyers Association of Abuja (RULAAC) has called for early legal access to detainees in Nigerian agencies, demanding reforms and accountability in the wake of the tragedy. In a statement, the group said the deaths have exposed systemic failures in the handling of suspects and detainees across the security sector.

No official findings have been released as of Tuesday, and the specific causes of death have not been confirmed by authorities.
