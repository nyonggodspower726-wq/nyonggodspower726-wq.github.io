ABIA STATE — Abia State Governor Alex Otti has declared he will neither become a political godfather nor impose a successor on the state after completing his second term in office in 2031, stating he does not tolerate such arrangements in Abia politics.

Otti made the declaration on Thursday in Umuahia while receiving the former Secretary to the State Government, whose name was not specified in the report, at the Government House.

"I don't tolerate godfathers, and I won't become one," Otti said during the reception. "I will not impose a successor on Abia people after my second term."

The governor's statement comes amid ongoing discussions about political succession in Abia State, where Otti is completing his first term after winning the 2023 governorship election under the Labour Party platform.

Otti emphasized that the decision about who will succeed him should rest with the people of Abia State, not with any individual or faction.

"The people of Abia will decide their next leader," he said. "Not me, not any godfather, not any faction."

The governor's stance positions him against the political godfatherism that has characterized Nigerian state politics for decades, where outgoing governors typically anoint their preferred successors.

Reactions to Otti's declaration were mixed across the state's political landscape. Some political analysts viewed the statement as a refreshing departure from traditional Nigerian politics, while others questioned its practical implications for the Labour Party's structure in Abia.

The declaration also adds a new dimension to the ongoing political calculations ahead of the 2027 and 2031 election cycles, as Otti has positioned himself as a reformist figure within Nigeria's opposition space.

Otti's refusal to act as a godfather aligns with his broader campaign promises of good governance and institutional reform. During his 2023 campaign, he promised to disrupt the status quo of Abia State politics, which had been dominated by what critics described as godfather-imposed administrations.

The governor's second term ends in 2031, meaning he will be term-limited out of office according to Abia State's constitutional provisions. His declaration effectively rules out any personal political project extending beyond his constitutional tenure.

Otti's position may also have implications for the wider Nigerian political space, where he has been mentioned as a potential national figure. His stance against godfatherism could become a reference point for other governors facing similar pressures from political stakeholders.

As of Thursday's declaration, no official successor has been named by the governor, and the Abia State Labour Party structure continues to operate under Otti's leadership without a designated anointed candidate for the post-2031 period.
