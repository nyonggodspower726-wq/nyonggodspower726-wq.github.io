The production marks a significant return for the operatic adaptation of Wole Soyinka’s 1960 play, originally staged at the University of Ife (now Obafemi Awolowo University). Vesta Orchestra, the ensemble behind the adaptation, has garnered critical acclaim for translating Soyinka’s stage directions and rhythmic language into operatic form, and this return engagement marks a significant moment for contemporary Nigerian opera.

‘The Trials of Brother Jero’, written by Wole Soyinka in 1960, premiered at the University of Ife in 1960 and has since been performed internationally. The opera adaptation by Vesta Orchestra represents a significant moment for contemporary Nigerian opera, translating Soyinka’s stage directions and rhythmic language into a musical format while retaining the biting social commentary that defines the source material. The return to the stage after a successful initial engagement signals a renewed interest in operatic interpretations of African literature and a renewed investment in homegrown theatrical works.

Set against the bustling backdrop of Lagos, the opera transposes Soyinka’s 1960s setting – a beachfront and the bustling wharf – into a contemporary context, while retaining the core narrative: a young beachboy, Jero, becomes entangled with a wealthy businessman and a religious prophet, forcing examinations of faith, power, and exploitation. The opera retains Soyinka’s sharp dialogue and rhythmic structure, translating them into musical form while updating the setting to reflect contemporary Lagos. This approach allows the work to resonate with contemporary audiences while preserving the core narrative arc and thematic depth of the original 1960 text.
