Manchester United manager Michael Carrick waded into the growing debate surrounding his side's display at Craven Cottage at the weekend, offering a robust defence of his players in the wake of Gary Neville's critical post-match analysis.

The draw - United's third consecutive Premier League game without a win - saw Lisandro Martinez contribute an own goal just over an hour in, giving Fulham the lead. Despite controlling 57% of possession and creating several opportunities, the Red Devils were held 1-1 by the Londoners, with Matheus Cunha's late equaliser earning a share of the spoils.

Speaking to BBC Sport after the match, Neville - the former United captain and now Sky Sports pundit - did not hold back. "I thought it was a laboured performance," Neville said. "I thought there was a lack of application, a lack of desire, a lack of quality in the final third. They looked like a team that hasn't got a plan."

Carrick, taking charge in an interim capacity following Erik ten Hag's departure, was asked for his response on Monday and offered a pointed rebuttal of the former England international's assessment.

"I think we did enough to win the game," Carrick said. "I think we could win it. It was an unfortunate own goal but for the most part we were good. Their goalkeeper has made top saves, on another day maybe we win the game."

The United manager acknowledged the frustration of the result but insisted the performance warranted more credit than the criticism suggested. "We created chances. We had the ball. We were dominant in large periods of the game. I thought the application and the desire was there," Carrick added.

Neville's comments came as part of a broader critique of United's tactical identity under Carrick, with the Sky Sports pundit questioning whether the side had a clear plan of play. The former England skipper has been particularly vocal about United's defensive vulnerabilities and lack of cutting edge in front of goal this season.

"Everyone is entitled to their opinion," Carrick said when pressed on Neville's specific points. "But I'm in the building every day. I see the work the players put in. I see the desire, the application, the quality in training. Sometimes the result doesn't reflect the performance, but I know what I see."

The draw leaves United sitting in the lower half of the table, and the result extended their winless run to three games. Yet Carrick remained upbeat, emphasizing that the team was building toward something more consistent.

"We're working on things. It's not going to happen overnight," he said. "But the spirit, the application, the desire - that's there. We just need to find a way to finish the chances we create."

The United hierarchy will be watching Carrick's handling of the situation closely, with the manager tasked with steadying a ship that has been adrift since the departure of Ten Hag in the summer. While Neville's comments have added fuel to the fire, Carrick's defence of his players suggests he intends to keep the focus internal rather than engage in a war of words with the punditry.

"We move on," Carrick said simply. "We've got a game coming up and we need to make sure we're ready."
